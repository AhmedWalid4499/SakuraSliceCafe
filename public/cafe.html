          Finding your way

          How the café works

          • W A S D or arrows to walk — or just click the floor
          • Drag to look around · scroll to come closer
          • Press 🪑 Sit (or click a chair or table) to sit at the closest free seat. Walk, or press Stand up, to get up.
          • Holding a drink? Press 🍵 Put drink down near a table — or click the table. Click your cup to pick it back up.
          • Click Hana or the counter (or ☕ Order) for a drink — or a dessert from the 🍡 Desserts tab. A plate goes in your other hand: 🍡 Take a bite, or set it down next to your cup.
          • Sitting at a table? Tick 🛎️ Bring it to my table on the menu and Hana or Yuki will carry it over. ☕ Take a sip (C) and 🍡 Take a bite (B) work on what's in your hands — or on your table.
          • 🎧 The café radio plays the songs the café chose — everyone hears the same song at the same moment. ▶ to listen, ⏸ to stop just for you.
          • Across the road, right opposite the front door: Koshari Abu Saied. Click the shop through the front windows to cross over, order a bowl at the counter, and sit at a marble table. And at the end of the street (best seen from the roof garden): the Nile, with feluccas, Gezira and Cairo Tower.
          • Every flat has its own door: Rooms 01–14 along the hallway at the top of the café's stairs, and Rooms 15–28 along the upper hallway, up the stairs at its far end (they carry on up to the roof garden).
          • At the west end of the upper hallway, a door to the stairs up to the second floor: the Sleepover Room — bunk beds and beds for everyone (click one to lie down), a bathroom with toilets, showers and sinks, a midnight-snack corner, and a big TV that anyone can put a YouTube video or playlist on; everyone in the room watches together.
          • The Sleepover Room is on the second floor landing, with its other rooms: the Games Room (pool, ping-pong and baby-foot — click a table to play, and your best goes on the high-score board — plus game tables for chess, tawla, snakes and ladders and Sakura Eights) and the BookBeans Library (armchairs, the author’s shelf of novellas, a coffee bar, and the writers’ nook: sit at a desk, start the timer and log your words on today’s board).
          • On the roof, through the door in the side of the stair house: the Rooftop Cinema — covered, so the films go on rain or shine. When a film is on the programme, everyone in there watches it together.
          • Also on the second floor: the Kitchen (a baking class — konafa, basbousa or sakura mochi, a step at a time, and it goes on your flat’s snack shelf), the Gym & Yoga Studio (treadmills and bikes, dumbbells, mats for a calm guided stretch and a mirror wall), the Art Studio (paint at an easel, then hang it on the gallery wall or in a frame in your flat) and the Laundry Room (put a wash on and watch it spin).
          • Across the road, next to the koshari shop: Ahwet El-Sett, the old coffee house — shai with mint, Turkish coffee or sahlab at the counter, tawla at the brass tables (🎲 Games), and Umm Kulthum on the gramophone.
          • Up on the roof, through the door on the other side of the stair house: the Ramadan Tent — khayamiya walls, lanterns, a sofra laid for iftar (dates, qamar el-din, qatayef), low tables for a game, the musaharati’s drum, and a board counting down to Maghrib in Ramadan (and to Ramadan the rest of the year). The cinema has a door on the second floor landing too.
          • More on the second floor: the Hammam & Spa (lie on the warm marble, take a steam — the screen fogs up — then a robe and a lounger), the Recording Studio (record a book chat or a story in the booth, and everyone in the studio can listen), the Japanese Tea Room (make matcha, step by step, on the tatami), the Kids’ Playroom (a ball pit, a tower of blocks, crayons, and Hana reading picture books) and the Post Room (a pigeonhole for every flat — write a letter to a neighbour and it waits for them).
          • By the café’s front door: the sign for the felucca on the Nile. It sails every few minutes from the landing, down past Cairo Tower and back — and carries everyone aboard with it.
          • Upstairs, your flat's balcony doors open and close — click them. And in the avatar maker you can pick pyjamas to wear in your room, the hallway, the Common room and the Music Room.
          • At the other end of the hallway: the Music Room (a piano and a guitar anyone can play, a cajón, a record player and an open-mic stage — sit on the stool to take it) and the Prayer Room, a quiet room with prayer mats facing the qibla and today's prayer times for Cairo. There's no chat in there.
          • Upstairs, the hallway's Common Room door (by the stairs to the roof) leads to the sofas and the big TV — it plays the café's playlist, and everyone watches together.
          • 🔗 at the top: invite a friend (they'll walk straight to your table) or take a 📸 photo of the café to share.
          • Sitting at a table? Press 🎲 Games to set out tawla or chess (play whoever sits across from you, or Yuto), snakes and ladders (2–4 players) or Sakura Eights, the café's own card game (2–6). Friends at the table sit in; Yuto, Hana, Yuki and Me3temed fill any empty seats. Anyone can walk over and watch.
          • Order at the counter and Hana calls your name when it's ready (if there's a line, you wait your turn). Every drink earns a stamp on your 🎟️ stamp card — ten stamps and Yuto brings you a slice of the sakura cake.
          • The menu board above the counter changes every day (click it to order); the A-frame by the counter has the week's specials. The easel by the fire says what's on — story evenings and book club.
          • The café follows the calendar: spring bunting, a summer bowl of lemons, autumn leaves, winter candles and blankets — and lanterns and later hours in Ramadan.
          • The photo wall on the left, between the windows, holds photos from the café — click one to see it bigger.
          • Click the bookshelf for the story shelf — every morning and bedtime story from the letters. The framed Book of the week hangs above it.
          • The tables are numbered 1 to 12 — Table 4 is the one by the window — and both front windows have a bar with stools for watching the street. The kitchen is through the back door: walk up and the doors swing open.
          • Any menu closes with its ✕, with Esc, or by tapping the café behind it. The chat has its own ✕ (💬 brings it back).
          • The clock and the weather are top left (tap them for more) — and on the street clock and chalkboards outside.
          • 📌 Leave a note on the board, a table, or a wall — everyone will see it
          • Click any note to read it and leave a ♥
          • 🗣️ Talk — or click Yuto, Hana, Yuki or Me3temed — to have a real conversation. Ask Hana what to drink, or ask anyone how to make it: the recipe comes as a card you can copy.
          • 🪜 Upstairs — or click the spiral stairs — for rooms just for your group.
          • E sit / stand · Q put down / pick up · C take a sip · B take a bite
          • Enter to chat · Esc to close things
